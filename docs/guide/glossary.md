# Glossary

Only words that appear on screen and are not obvious from where they appear. Everything else is
explained on the page where it is used.

**Adjustment** — Taking stock off the shelf, or putting it on, without a sale or a delivery.
Used for damage, loss, or correcting a mistake. References start **AD_**.

**Average cost** — What a product has cost the shop on average, weighted across everything
bought. It changes when you book in a delivery at a different price, and it is what every margin
and stock valuation is worked out against. Not the shelf price.

**Expected cash** — What should be in the drawer: the opening float, plus cash sales, plus
**Cash in**, less **Cash out**, less cash refunds.

**Float** — The cash a drawer starts the shift with, so there is change available before
anything is sold.

**Goods receipt** — The record of a delivery actually arriving and being accepted. Separate from
the purchase order, because what arrives is often not what was ordered. References start
**GRN_**.

**Purchase order** — What you asked a supplier to send. It changes no stock on its own.
References start **PR_**.

**Reorder quantity** — How much to buy when a product runs low. Used to work out **Suggested
Qty** on the reorder screen.

**Short-closed** — A purchase order deliberately finished with part of it never delivered,
because the balance is not coming. Keeps it out of the list of goods still expected.

**Stock alert quantity** — The level a product should not drop below. When it does, the product
appears on the reorder and alert screens. A product left at 0 never appears, however empty it
gets.

**Terminal** — The name of a counter. A shop with two tills gives each one a name so the two
drawers can be told apart.

**Till session** — One shift on one drawer: opened with a float, closed with a count. Everything
sold in between belongs to it.

**Transfer** — Moving stock between Main Warehouse and Branch Warehouse. References start
**TR_**.

**VAT-inclusive** — The price already contains the 12% VAT, so the figure on the shelf is what
the customer pays. The VAT is worked back out of it on the receipt. This is how Philippine retail
prices are shown.

**Void** — Cancelling a sale made today, on the current shift. The stock goes back, the money
comes off the drawer, and the invoice is kept and marked voided rather than deleted.

**Z-reading** — The end-of-day record for a drawer: float, cash counted, the difference, the
invoice range, the VAT split and any voids. Produced when the till is closed and not editable
afterwards.

---

This list is short on purpose. A long glossary usually means the screens are using words the
people operating them would not — which is a problem with the product rather than the guide. The
entries above are the ones that genuinely could not be made obvious in place.
